Dear Mr. DeJoy and distinguished Board Members:
We write to share our concerns about the initial results of the Mail Processing Facility Review
(MPFR) at the Macon, Georgia Processing and Distribution Center (P&DC). The downsizing of
the Macon mail processing facility would undoubtedly lead to decreased service standards and
the loss of middle-class jobs, both career and non-career, for the people of our districts.
We share your belief that every zip code, household, and business in America deserves the U.S.
Postal Service’s highest service standards, and we are concerned that America’s rural areas and
small towns are being left behind. Over the past few decades, rural and small urban communities
in our districts have seen their postal facilities consistently scaled down and consolidated with
processing operations in highly populated areas, either out-of-state or in the Atlanta region.
Following the closings of the Columbus, Albany, and Valdosta Customer Service Mail
Processing Centers (CSMPCs) in 2012, the communities we represent lost jobs and faced
delivery delays due to the routing of local mail and packages through distant processing
facilities. While we understand that the U.S. Postal Service is facing acute financial difficulties
and as a result has begun implementation of the Delivering for America Plan, we are concerned
that the transformation of the Macon P&DC into a Local Processing Center (LPC), which, as we
understand, would handle only letters and flat mail rather than packages, could lead to further
customer service disruptions in the rural towns and small urban communities that depend on the
Postal Service the most.
