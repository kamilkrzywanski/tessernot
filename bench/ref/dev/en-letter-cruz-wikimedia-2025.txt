Dear Ms. Iskander:
I write to request information about ideological bias on the Wikipedia platform and at the
Wikimedia Foundation.
Wikipedia began with a noble concept: crowdsource human knowledge using verifiable sources
and make it free to the public. That’s what makes reports of Wikipedia’s systemic bias especially
troubling. Wikipedia is one of the most visited websites in the world,¹ and its articles appear in
more than 80 percent of desktop search results for common queries, according to Northwestern
University researchers.² Its influence extends even further in the age of artificial intelligence, as
every major large language model has been trained on the platform.³ Wikipedia shapes what
Americans read today and what technology will produce tomorrow.
Wikipedia says that its articles must be written from a neutral point of view (NPOV). But as
Wikipedia’s co-founder Larry Sanger argues, “Wikipedia’s ‘NPOV’ is dead.”⁴ As Sanger tells it,
most Wikipedia editors’ “notion of what is credible” biases them against “conservatism,
traditional religiosity, and minority perspectives on science and medicine.”⁵ And indeed,
