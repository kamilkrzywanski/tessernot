Dear Mr. Warrington:
I write regarding alarming reports that President Trump intends to accept a luxury
airplane as a gift from the Qatari ruling family—a transaction that, according to one leading
ethics expert, “would probably be the largest gift given to a U.S. president by a foreign
government in modern history.”¹ The gift of this aircraft—reportedly worth $400 million—to
the President by a foreign state, absent congressional approval, would clearly violate the
Constitution’s Foreign Emoluments Clause.² In addition to the profoundly irresponsible,
reckless and dangerous national security concerns that this arrangement raises, this proposed
deal, if executed, would be patently illegal and corrupt and would advertise to our allies and
adversaries that the President of the United States simply cannot resist an opportunity to enrich
himself and has placed the White House up for sale.³ The Trump Administration must conduct
U.S. foreign policy in the interest of the American people— and not the interests of foreign
governments that enrich the President. I urge you to immediately decline this corrupt, foreign
gift.
Our nation’s forefathers were deeply wary from their experience under a tyrannical king
and restricted foreign emoluments in the Constitution. The Framers did this to ensure that the
President prioritizes the interests of the United States above any personal financial interests and
is not tempted to jeopardize U.S. national or economic security for his own personal
