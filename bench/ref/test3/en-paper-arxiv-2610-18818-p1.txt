ABSTRACT
Pre-trained Large Language Model (LLMs) are effective on many tasks in zero-
shot and few-shot settings. For machine translation of low-resource languages,
the obvious options are few-shot prompting and fine-tuning on a small parallel
set. We ask: what if moderate amounts of low-resource monolingual data are also
available? We argue that, rather than continued pretraining, a more effective use of
this data is the pre-LLM era technique of back-translation (BT). The current fine-
tuned LLM translates monolingual sentences in each language, and the resulting
pairs train it in the reverse direction. Updates on the parallel set are interleaved for
stable learning. With 1K parallel pairs on Odia and Swahili, BT beats continued
pretraining into the low-resource language by 7.7 to 16.4 XCOMET-XXL points in
domain. With beam-decoded intermediate translations, it also beats a model with
10× more parallel data. In-context examples retrieved from the parallel data further
improve BT. We also analyze the source, quality and amount of monolingual text.
Across five low-resource languages, with Hindi as a high-resource control, and two
3B backbones, BT improves translation into the low-resource language by 16 to
173% XCOMET-XXL over parallel-only fine-tuning. Blind human evaluation on
Odia and Sanskrit agrees with the automatic metrics.
1 INTRODUCTION
LLMs are effective on many tasks in zero-shot and few-shot settings (Brown et al., 2020), and they
translate well between high-resource languages (Hendy et al., 2023). They are, however, pre-trained
overwhelmingly on a handful of high-resource languages, while most of the world’s languages
appear only sparsely. Open-source instruction-tuned models are therefore fluent in English but have
weak or near-zero competence in many low-resource languages. Gold-standard parallel corpora
for low-resource pairs are also typically two to three orders of magnitude smaller than those for
high-resource pairs, and they are often confined to narrow domains (Ramesh et al., 2022; Gala et al.,
2023; NLLB Team et al., 2022).
The obvious ways to use such model for a low-resource language are few-shot prompting and
supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a parallel set. Few-shot prompting places a few translated examples
in the prompt, and retrieving examples that are similar to the input helps further (Vilar et al., 2023;
Zebaze et al., 2025a;b). SFT on a small, high-quality parallel set adapts an LLM to translation (Xu
et al., 2024). Both depend on what the model already knows about the language. But what if we have
a moderate amount of monolingual text available at our disposal. So, this paper asks: Can an LLM
with a weak prior leverage monolingual text for better translation, and how should that text enter
training?
