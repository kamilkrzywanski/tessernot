ABSTRACT
Accurately assessing the convergence of Monte Carlo Markov Chains is crucial for ensuring their va-
lidity in Bayesian inference problems. However, convergence assessment in high dimensions is known
to be difficult, due to most convergence diagnostics becoming insensitive beyond a few dozen param-
eters. Conventional convergence statistics analyze marginal distributions which in high dimensions
neglects increasing levels of information from the joint and conditional probability distributions. Thus
we propose NFDiag, which taps into the information of the full multivariate joint posterior with a nor-
malizing flow (NF). Our convergence diagnostic thereby also analyzes information from conditional
distributions. In other words, by working in the full multivariate space, it is sensitive with respect to
the geometry of the chain in high dimensions. Our diagnostic utilizes that NFs assume independent
and identically distributed (iid) training samples, which is a testable assumption that will be violated
by a non-converged or strongly correlated chain. Our diagnostic trains NFs on separate parts of a
chain, and then assesses whether these correspond to the same multivariate target distribution. We
test our diagnostic in up to 250 dimensions, and demonstrate it is universal and unbiased, and detects
problematic chain quality also when Gelman-Rubin R̂ and the multivarite effective sample size do not.
1. INTRODUCTION
Bayesian statistics (Bayes 1763; Fienberg 2006) pro-
vides a practical framework for solving scientific infer-
ence problems. While some Bayesian inference prob-
lems can be solved completely analytically, most rely
heavily on computational methods (Gelman et al. 2013),
such as Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) (Metropo-
lis et al. 1953; Hastings 1970) algorithms. Following the
initial Metropolis-Hastings algorithm, more variations of
MCMC were developed, such as the Gibbs sampler (Ge-
man & Geman 1984; Gelfand & Smith 1990), the Hamil-
tonian Monte Carlo (HMC) sampler (Duane et al. 1987),
piecewise deterministic samplers (Bertazzi & Bierkens
2022), the No U-Turn sampler (NUTS) (Hoffman & Gel-
man 2014) and the affine invariant sampler (Goodman &
Weare 2010).
As these algorithms allowed for sampling from more
complex and increasingly higher dimensional probability
distributions, a typical research question became: How
can one tell that a chain is of sufficient quality to faith-
fully represent the target distribution? This is usually
abbreviated to the question of whether the chain has con-
verged.
In dimensions beyond a few dozen, this question be-
comes increasingly hard to answer, and this paper hence
proposes a diagnostic for chain quality assessment in high
dimensions.
One prerequisite for convergence is that the chain has
sufficiently mixed and explored all the subvolumes of
the probability distribution. This is implicitly a ques-
tion about the geometric shape of the chain in high di-
mensions. This geometry contains valuable information
about whether the chain correctly resolves the condition-
als of the target distribution.

The most popular convergence diagnostics analyze
marginals of the chain only. These include visual in-
spection of marginal scatter plots, the correlation length
of the chain and the effective sample size (ESS) (Geyer
1992), Gelman-Rubin’s R̂ (Gelman & Rubin 1992) and
Hanson’s diagnostic (Hanson 2001)).
Restricting the analysis to marginals only loses in-
formation. For example, unexplored sub-volumes typ-
ically become invisible when projecting down onto a
2-dimensional marginal, and a convoluted trace of the
chain in the full volume can look like a well-mixed chain
in a 2-dimensional projection. In the following, we there-
fore train normalizing flows (NFs) to use the informa-
tion in the conditional distributions on top of that in the
marginals.
We utilize NFs to construct the posterior of samples
from an MCMC chain. NFs assume the samples used to
train its neural networks to be independent and identi-
cally distributed (iid), and a chain that is yet to converge
contradicts this assumption. Thus a NF model built with
samples falsely assuming iid will fail to accurately rep-
resent another chain from the same target distribution.
Our code NFDiag harnesses this fact to render the lack
of convergence in MCMC chains visible. It computes
a new convergence statistic in high dimensions we have
called NFD, given by Eq. 8, which is the main result of
this paper.
